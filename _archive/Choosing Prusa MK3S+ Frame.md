# Choosing Prusa MK3S+ Frame

**Human:**
I have all the parts for a Prusa MK3S+ except for the frame.  Should I buy the standard MK3 Frame or buy a Bear 2.1 frame?

***

**Assistant:**

The decision between a standard Prusa MK3S+ frame and a Bear 2.1 frame for your 3D printer depends on your specific needs, preferences, and budget. Here's a brief comparison of the two:

1. 
<strong>Standard Prusa MK3S+ Frame</strong>: This is the original frame designed by Prusa for the MK3S+. It's been widely tested and used, so it's reliable and you know it will fit your parts exactly. It's made from laser cut aluminum, which is fairly rigid and suitable for most printing tasks. This is also typically the more cost-effective option.

2. 
<strong>Bear 2.1 Frame</strong>: The Bear upgrade, on the other hand, is designed for users who want to improve their printer's rigidity and stability. It is made with extruded aluminum profiles, which can be more rigid and reduce vibrations, potentially leading to better print quality. It also gives the printer a slightly different aesthetic look and it's often cited as being more sturdy and durable. However, it's typically more expensive and you might need additional components not included in the standard Prusa MK3S+ kit.



Factors to consider:

- 
<strong>Print Quality</strong>: If you're seeking the best possible print quality and don't mind the extra cost and effort, the Bear 2.1 upgrade can be a good choice due to its increased rigidity and stability.

- 
<strong>Budget</strong>: If you're trying to keep costs down, the standard Prusa MK3S+ frame might be the better option.

- 
<strong>Assembly</strong>: Keep in mind that changing the frame can also change the assembly process. Both frames will come with assembly instructions, but if you're a beginner, the standard frame might be easier to assemble.

- 
<strong>Compatibility</strong>: Ensure all your parts are compatible with the frame you choose. This is typically not an issue, but it's always a good idea to double-check.



In summary, both options are valid and have their advantages. It depends on what you value most in your 3D printer.
